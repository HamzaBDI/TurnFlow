How the training algorithm works

TurnFlow schedules each move in your repertoire on its own timeline, based on how well you know it — a spaced repetition system (SRS). The better you know a line, the longer TurnFlow waits before testing it again.

1. Learning a new (or relearning a lapsed) line

The first time you see a move — or any time you get it wrong — it starts in a short learning phase, tested again soon on the same day rather than being scheduled far into the future:

  • Correct, 1st time: retested 20 minutes later.
  • Correct again at that 20-minute check: the line graduates and moves to the day-based schedule below.
  • Used a hint or struggled (several retries): retested in 10 minutes, learning phase restarts.
  • Wrong or revealed the answer: retested in 5 minutes, learning phase restarts.

2. Graduated lines: day-based intervals

Once a line graduates, each further correct answer pushes its next review further away:

  • 1st graduation: next review in 1 day.
  • Next correct review: interval becomes 3 days.
  • Every correct review after that: the interval is multiplied by ×1.5 and rounded (e.g. 3 → 5 → 7 → 11 days…), so well-known lines get reviewed less and less often.
  • Any mistake at this stage — wrong move, hint, or struggle — drops the line straight back into the learning phase (step 1), so it doesn't keep a long interval it hasn't earned.

3. What counts as "due"

A line appears in your Due (SRS) queue as soon as the current time passes its scheduled review time — whether that's 20 minutes from now or weeks away. The Training tab shows what's due right now, and flags what's due later today so you know it's coming without it cluttering your session.

4. How the dashboard labels a line

  • Fresh: never attempted yet.
  • Struggling: wrong on more than 40% of attempts, or 3+ wrong attempts total.
  • Mastered: interval has grown to 14 days or more.
  • Learning: everything in between — including lines still in the 20-minute learning phase.
